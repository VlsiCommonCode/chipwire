AXI vs TileLink.

Two interconnect contracts that show up in almost every open SoC. Pick the one that matches the cores and IP you actually have.

What they are

AXI (AMBA) is the industry-default memory-mapped bus: independent address/data channels, explicit burst types, and a huge catalog of commercial IP. Most SystemVerilog SoCs and FPGA interconnects speak AXI or AXI-Lite.

TileLink is the coherent protocol used by Rocket Chip, BOOM, and Chipyard. It is built around cached agents, probe/release, and a smaller set of message types. If you are composing Chisel generators, TileLink is the native fabric.

When to use which

Use AXI when you need to drop in third-party DMA, PCIe, or DRAM controllers and stay compatible with ARM-shaped IP. Use TileLink when the CPU, L1/L2, and MMIO devices are generated together and you want coherency without an ACE-shaped bolt-on.

Bridges exist in both directions (TileLink–AXI in Rocket/Chipyard, AXI fabrics in LiteX and OpenHW SoCs). The engineering cost is usually in ordering and cache semantics, not in wiring.
